# Harborline Public Services | Security awareness program and simulated phishing results

**Prepared by Cierra Jennings** · **Exercise ID AW-01** · **Fictional training and scripted results**

## Program design

Harborline trains staff to recognize suspicious requests, independently verify them, and report promptly. All new staff complete onboarding training within 30 days. All active staff receive an annual refresher and quarterly short practice. Security Awareness owns the program. People Operations tracks assignments and accessibility needs, and Security Operations owns the reporting and acknowledgement workflow.

| Audience | Learning objective | Evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| All staff | Recognize unexpected requests and report through the approved channel | Practice selecting a reporting action in a training exercise |
| Customer support | Verify requests for municipal or resident records using a known contact method | Choose a safe response to a mock data-sharing request |
| Engineering and administrators | Verify unexpected access and approval requests through a trusted system | Explain when to deny, report, and escalate a request |
| Managers | Reinforce reporting, help staff verify requests, and escalate patterns | Review aggregate metrics and document corrective coaching |

## Simulated phishing campaign

Run the exercise as an offline mock inbox in an approved learning environment. Participants choose whether to report, independently verify, ignore, or proceed when shown a fictional suspicious request. Use synthetic identities and static mock-ups. Record choices only. The exercise sends no email, uses no live links or attachments, and collects no credentials.

Before a real training event, obtain approval from Security, Privacy and People Operations. Document the scope, audience, accessible alternatives, reporting channel, and data handling. Debrief participants promptly, report aggregate results, and give private coaching without naming individuals in leadership reports. The planning assumption is to retain identifiable learning records for up to 90 days under approved training rules.

**Measurement.** Completion is participants divided by assigned learners. Reporting and unsafe-choice rates use participants as the denominator. An unsafe choice means selecting the option to proceed without independent verification in the mock-up. Reports and unsafe choices are separate behaviors. Exercise targets are at least 95% completion, at least 60% reporting, and at most 10% unsafe choices.

## Results report

These numbers are scripted for the portfolio. No live campaign occurred.

| Metric | Baseline | Follow-up | Target | Follow-up result |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Completion | 92/100 · 92.0% | 97/100 · 97.0% | ≥95% | Met |
| Reporting | 36/92 · 39.1% | 59/97 · 60.8% | ≥60% | Met |
| Unsafe choice | 17/92 · 18.5% | 10/97 · 10.3% | ≤10% | Missed |

Reporting rose about 21.7 percentage points. The unsafe-choice rate fell about 8.2 percentage points and narrowly missed target. Customer support had the highest follow-up unsafe-choice rate, 4 of 24 participants, compared with 4 of 38 in Operations and 2 of 35 in Engineering. Participation changed between rounds. Treat the comparison as a descriptive exercise result.

## Corrective action and retest

Security Awareness owns a customer-support refresher and a repeat offline exercise by **February 15, 2027**, the fictional due date for SEC-02. The refresher should cover verification through known contact channels and practice using the reporting workflow. Security Assurance should confirm the cohort population and counts, independently recalculate rates, and record whether all three targets are met. Keep SEC-02 open until an authorized exercise produces evidence supporting a verified residual assessment.

The workbook links AW-01 to SEC-02 and ISO Annex A A.6.3 and A.6.8, and records the cohort counts behind this report. No organizational effectiveness or certification is asserted.
