# Harborline Public Services | Sample security policy suite

**Prepared by Cierra Jennings** · **Version 0.1** · **Draft, pending approval**  
**Scope** Harborline’s fictional municipal casework portal, workforce, managed devices, information, and third parties with authorized access.  
**Policy owner** Security Governance · **Approver** COO after Security, Legal, and Privacy review  
**Review cycle** Annually and after a material change or incident. The policy owner records approval, effective date, distribution, and exceptions.

## P-01 · Acceptable Use Policy

**Purpose.** Protect municipal records and Harborline systems from unauthorized access, disclosure, and misuse.

- Use approved devices, accounts, software, storage, and transfer channels. Keep resident data out of personal email, unmanaged storage, and unapproved external services. Use synthetic records for training and demonstrations.
- Use your own account and approved authentication method. Keep credentials, tokens, and recovery codes private. Lock your device when unattended.
- Access only the records needed for your assigned duties. Confirm the recipient and approved channel before sharing restricted information. Obtain Security and Privacy approval for a new sharing purpose or tool.
- Report a lost device, suspicious message, misdirected record, or unexpected account activity promptly using the approved reporting channel. Preserve the message or record and follow Security’s instructions.
- Security may review activity for authorized protective purposes under approved procedures. People Operations handles suspected misuse with Security and Legal.

**Verification.** Security Governance reconciles annual acknowledgements to the active workforce, samples approved transfer records, and reviews exceptions. Related workbook records include A.5.10 and SEC-10.

## P-02 · Incident Response Policy

**Purpose.** Detect, contain, investigate, and recover from security incidents while preserving evidence and protecting municipal customers.

- Anyone who suspects an incident reports it immediately through the approved channel. If that channel is unavailable, use the on-call route in the response directory. Staff preserve relevant messages or records and wait for direction before changing affected systems.
- Security Operations records the reporter, timestamps, systems, known data categories, and initial scope. The Incident Response Lead assigns severity, opens an incident record, and appoints an incident coordinator. Suspected exposure of restricted data or sustained disruption escalates immediately to the on-call lead.
- The coordinator assigns containment and recovery actions. Preserve logs and other relevant records in restricted storage. Record source, collector, collection time, integrity verification where possible, access and transfers. Coordinate actions that could disrupt municipal services with the service owner.
- Legal and Privacy review reporting duties and deadlines against the relevant agreement and applicable rules. The municipal account lead communicates approved facts, material unknowns, actions and next update time. The response directory records primary and backup vendor contacts.
- Restore services only after the service owner and Incident Response Lead confirm recovery criteria and monitoring. Within ten business days after the incident stabilizes, hold a lessons-learned review and assign corrective actions with due dates and verification criteria.

**Verification.** Security Assurance reviews exercise records at least annually and after a material change. Sample classification, escalation, evidence handling, communications, and recovery decisions. Related records include A.5.24–A.5.28 and SEC-09.

## P-03 · Access Control Policy

**Purpose.** Give people and service identities only the access needed for approved work and remove it when that need ends.

- Each person uses a unique identity. A supervisor requests access, the data or application owner approves it, and Identity Security records the role, scope, reason and expiration. A requester cannot approve their own privileged access.
- Require MFA for workforce and privileged access. Use separate administrative identities, approved credential storage, and the narrowest available privileges. Assign named owners to service identities and review their credentials and permissions.
- Time-limit privileged and third-party access. Review privileged and vendor roles quarterly and standard roles at least semiannually. Record each decision and remove or reapprove unjustified access.
- Review access at job changes and remove privileges no longer needed. Disable access at the effective separation time or contractor end date. Revoke active sessions and tokens as applicable. Reconcile exit records against directory and application evidence.
- Emergency access requires a recorded reason, time limit and post-use review by someone independent of the requester. Security Operations monitors emergency use and routes unexpected activity to incident response.

**Verification.** Security Assurance compares the full privileged and vendor role list to source approvals and samples revocation timestamps, MFA coverage and emergency access review. Related records include A.5.15–A.5.18, A.8.2, SEC-01, and SEC-03.

## Approval, rollout, and exceptions

Security Governance tracks each policy through draft, stakeholder review, approval, publication, acknowledgement, and verification. Record the approved version, effective date, distribution evidence and test reference. An exception requires an owner, scope, justification, compensating controls, expiration, and documented Security and accountable executive approval. Review before expiration and record the final disposition.
